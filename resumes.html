Resumes


New to resume writing? This guide will help you through every section of creating your resume.

How to use this guide:

Click any section to expand it. Each one explains what the section is, what to include (and what not to), and shows a real example from an allied health resume. Start from the top and work your way down.

1
Contact Information
Your name, credentials, and how employers reach you
Required

This is the very top of your resume. It's the first thing the hiring manager sees, so it needs to be clean, correct, and professional. Think of this as your business card.

Include: Your full name, credential, city and state, a professional email, and your phone number.
Leave off: your full home address, a photo, your age or date of birth, personal emails, and social media profiles.

2
Professional Summary
A 2 to 3 sentence snapshot of who you are and your experience
Highly Recommended

A brief paragraph at the top that tells employers who you are, what you specialize in, and what you bring to the role. Tailor it slightly for each job application.

Example: "Compassionate Medical Assistant with 4+ years of experience in outpatient clinical settings. Skilled in phlebotomy, EHR documentation, and patient education."

Include: your years of experience, key clinical skills, career goal or specialty, and a standout quality.
Leave off: "I" statements, vague filler phrases, salary expectations, and anything longer than 4 to 5 sentences.

3
Work Experience
Your work experience from the last 10 to 15 years, in reverse order
Required

List your jobs starting with the most recent. For each role include your job title, employer, city and state, dates, and 3–5 bullet points describing your responsibilities and accomplishments. For example: Medical Assistant — Valley Health Clinic, Oakland, CA — Jan 2020–Present.

Include: job title, employer, location, dates, action verb bullet points, and quantified achievements.
Leave off: reasons for leaving, jobs older than 15 years, paragraph-style descriptions, and supervisor contact info.

4
Education
Your degrees, diplomas, and training programs
Required

List your highest level of education first. Include the school name, degree or certificate earned, and graduation year. You don't need to list your GPA unless it's 3.5 or higher. For example: Certificate in Medical Assisting — Chabot College, Hayward, CA — 2019.

Include: degree or certificate name, school name and location, year completed, and relevant coursework if applicable.
Leave off: high school if you have college, GPA under 3.5, and incomplete programs unless currently enrolled.

5
Licenses and Certifications
Your state certifications and professional credentials
Required

This section is especially important in allied health. List every current license and certification including the issuing body and expiration date. For example: Certified Medical Assistant (CMA) — AAMA, Exp. 2026 | BLS/CPR — American Red Cross, Exp. 2025.

Include: the full credential name and acronym, issuing organization, expiration date, and license number if required.
Leave off: expired certifications, irrelevant certificates, and online hobby courses.

6
Skills
A concise list of your key competencies and skills
Required

A scannable list of your top clinical and professional skills. Use keywords from the job description — this helps your resume pass applicant tracking systems (ATS). For example: Phlebotomy • EKG/ECG • Epic EMR • Vital Signs • Patient Education • Bilingual (Spanish/English).

Include: clinical and technical skills, software and EMR systems, languages spoken, and a few soft skills.
Leave off: Skills you can't demonstrate and more than 12 to 15 items total.
